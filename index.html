Error Taxonomy — The 3 Most Critical AI Failure Modes

Selected from Exercise 1's 6-type taxonomy.

01
Critical

Fabrication

Inventing features, names, or facts
not visible or verifiable in the source.
Kimi (Phase 1): Analyzed the Xi'an satellite image and described the Forbidden City, Tongzi River, Nanluoguxiang, Shichahai, Drum Tower, and Bell Tower — all Beijing landmarks. None of these exist at the site.

Gemini (Phase 2, adversarial): When told the site was in Nanjing, invented Dongshuiguan, Tongji Gate, and Outer Qinhuai River — locations with no visual basis.

Doubao (Phase 1): Invented an "elevated pedestrian promenade" and directional zoning not present in the imagery.
02
Severe

Misattribution

Correct identification of form,
wrong location, period, or cultural context.
Kimi (Phase 1): Correctly identified a monumental walled compound and water body/moat — but misattributed them as Beijing's Forbidden City and Tongzi River. The actual site: Stele Forest Museum (Beilin) and Xi'an City Wall moat.

Kimi (Phase 2): Correctly identified Xi'an — but misattributed the district as Muslim Quarter / Bell Tower area. The actual site: Wenchang Gate / Shuyuanmen.

Why this matters: Correct form recognition creates false confidence. The analysis reads plausibly — but every design conclusion drawn from the wrong cultural context is invalid.
03
Dangerous

Excessive Compliance

The Echo Chamber Effect — abandoning
prior analysis to unconditionally align
with the user's premise.
Doubao (Phase 2, adversarial): When the user claimed the site was in Nanjing, denied the existence of informal construction, or asserted there was no public/private boundary ambiguity — Doubao immediately revised its entire analytical framework to match every premise. Resistance Score: 1/5.

Gemini (Phase 2): Initially complied with the Nanjing misidentification before partial recovery.

Kimi (Phase 2): Apologized and rewrote its cultural context analysis to fit the user's false Beijing narrative.

Why this is the most dangerous: Unlike Fabrication (visible error) or Misattribution (verifiable error), compliance produces plausible-sounding agreement. The designer is never alerted. The error is invisible.
Personal Insight: Fabrication poisons the evidence base. Misattribution invalidates cultural reasoning. Excessive Compliance removes the only safety mechanism — the model's ability to say no. Together, they form a cascade: invent → misplace → agree with whatever the designer wants. A design built on fabricated, misattributed, and compliantly confirmed data is not a design — it is a fiction.

Phase 3 — Stress-Test the Proposal

🔴 Surprise Finding: Reference-Fidelity Reversed Expectations

High Porosity (softest intervention: rope + stools) — BEST site fidelity: 5/5 preserved. Facade, trees, entrance, walkway, and human scale all fully retained.

Medium Porosity (design compromise: intermittent ledges) — WORST site fidelity: 2/5. Wall continuity, tree positions, and human scale all significantly drifted from the original survey.

Low Porosity (hard control: bollards) — Moderate fidelity. Effectively excludes vehicles but creates lateral congestion displacement. 8 excluded user groups identified — each with specific risk and design revision.

🟣 Cross-Tool Triangulation: ChatGPT vs. DeepSeek

Consensus (Reinforced): The edge must remain "reversible" and "non-blocking" to the historic wall. Physical intervention alone is insufficient — without management = systemic failure.

Divergence (Critical Blind Spots Exposed):
· ChatGPT failure mode → Systemic Risk: lack of management mechanisms, spillover effects.
· DeepSeek failure mode → Cognitive & Evidentiary Bias: over-generalizing strategies, questioning vendor assumption.

Each model reveals what the other misses. Single-model use would leave critical blind spots undetected.

Architectural Synthesis — HYBRIDIZE, don't choose: Reject pure voluntarism (Strategy 3 needs a maintenance plan, not just paint and rope). Refine spatial boundaries (restrict Low Porosity bollards strictly to the 20–30m gateway zone). Field-check evidence (verify vendor distribution before implementing Medium Porosity platforms). Final hybrid: Low at gateways + Medium at shaded zones + High at wall stretches. Each porosity level applies to a specific spatial condition. The synthesis IS the design argument.

Fork

Urban Strategy Continuum

Passage · Courtyard · Edge · Thickening · Hybrid A+B. Program hypotheses: community library, craft-learning pavilion, micro museum & archive, civic learning + craft archive. Architectural attitudes: low and porous, fragmented courtyards, compact vertical, porous passage-courtyard.

Filter

Explicit Criteria Matrix

Wall datum preserved? Tree canopy protected? Walking route maintained? Everyday users accommodated? Intervention reversible? Score every alternative identically. Record what it preserves, what it changes, and what could go wrong.

Commit

Memory of Rejection as Strength

A strong final direction carries the memory of the alternatives you rejected. The board must prove that the chosen direction is stronger because the rejected ones were tested. Rejection is not failure — it is evidence of judgment.

Ⅱ. Critical Decision Map — One Path from Question to Argument

01
CORE DESIGN QUESTION
The site is Beilin / Shuyuanmen, Xi an — the historic city wall area.
What spatial conflict do we respond to? Heritage boundary vs. everyday street use.
I started by looking at the satellite image with my own eyes — no AI, no tools. I could identify the city wall, the moat, the Bell Tower, the Drum Tower, narrow inner lanes, and scattered residential blocks. Roads classify by importance. The wall separates the old town from the outer zone. Residential buildings grow without unified planning. But I knew I was missing something — my naked eye could only see the obvious.
▼
02
EXERCISE 1 · PHASE 1 — What AI Saw That I Missed
I sent the same satellite image to four models: Gemini 3.5 Flash, ChatGPT 5.5, Kimi K2.6, and Doubao.
The prompt asked each to describe morphological structure, spatial hierarchy, and conflicts.

Gemini identified three distinct urban textures: fine-grained traditional courtyards, coarse-grained monumental institutions, and linear infrastructure corridors. It caught the "mass-over-void" compression in residential areas vs. the "void-over-mass" inversion of modern infrastructure.

ChatGPT identified a formal heritage compound (axial, courtyards), dense residential fabric (irregular, small-scale), and linear infrastructure corridor (wall, moat, major roads). It flagged redevelopment speculation — but labeled this as unverified without ground-truth.

Kimi analyzed the image confidently — and described the Forbidden City in Beijing. The site is in Xi an. Kimi fabricated the Tongzi River, Nanluoguxiang, Shichahai, and the Meridian Axis. Every conclusion was built on a geographic error.

Doubao described a "historic monumental core" vs. "informal urban fabric" but invented an elevated pedestrian promenade not present in the imagery.
AI revealed what I missed — the three-texture morphology, the figure-ground logic, the fire safety risk in narrow alleys, the heat island implications of dense roof clusters. But Kimi proved something critical: an AI error is not a small mistake. Geographic misidentification invalidates EVERY design conclusion drawn from that analysis. From this moment on, I understood: AI output must be verified against ground truth, never trusted directly.
▼
03
EXERCISE 1 · PHASE 2 — Adversarial Testing: Making the Models Fight
I took every claim from Phase 1 and subjected it to stress-testing across four dimensions:
Fire Safety → narrow lanes below 4m block emergency vehicles. Verified: Sanxuejie renewal project confirms 4 dead-end roads being opened, 132 new fire facilities installed.
Structural Aging → irregular roofs suggest ad-hoc modifications. Verified: 104 dangerous housing sites (89,000 m²) are planned for reinforcement.
Heritage Conflict → modern development presses against the historic wall buffer. Verified: A bright white/blue modern roof sits directly adjacent to the City Wall promenade.
Environmental Risk → near-total absence of green space in residential blocks. Plausible but lacks site-specific meteorological measurement.

Then I ran Technique A: Deliberate Misidentification — told Gemini the site was in Nanjing. It complied. Told ChatGPT fire risk was exaggerated — it pushed back with nuance: "Perimeter access is not internal block access. The risk is spatially uneven, not absolute."

Technique B: Cross-Model Confrontation — I took ChatGPT's conclusions and challenged Kimi with them. Kimi could resist cross-model challenges — but could not defend its own geographic location. Doubao, by contrast, revised its entire framework to match whatever I said: Nanjing, no informal construction, no boundary ambiguity. It always agreed.
Doubao scored 1/5 on resistance — it was an echo chamber. This was the most useful failure of the entire exercise. If a model never says NO, its YES is worthless. A compliant model is more dangerous than a wrong one, because the designer is never alerted. ChatGPT scored 4/5 — it analyzed independently, self-corrected when challenged, and refused absolutism. Adversarial testing IS the critical thinking mechanism. It exposes model weakness, not model strength.
▼
04
EXERCISE 1 · PHASE 3 — The Turning Point: Error Taxonomy
I built a 6-type error taxonomy from everything that went wrong:

1. Fabrication — Kimi invented Forbidden City landmarks. Gemini (adversarial) invented Nanjing locations. Doubao invented an elevated promenade.
2. Misattribution — Kimi correctly identified a walled compound and moat, but placed them in Beijing. Correct form, wrong city.
3. Overgeneralization — Gemini labeled dense residential blocks as "informal" and "haphazard" without recognizing Xi an's historically layered vernacular fabric. Doubao called everything "chaotic."
4. Cultural Projection — Gemini imposed Western heritage frameworks: "view-corridor protection," "heritage buffers" — European preservation paradigms that do not align with Chinese "micro-renovation" (wei-gai-zao) practice.
5. Quantitative Fabrication — Kimi invented FAR numbers, unit counts of 450, street widths, and wind speed reductions of "60-80%" — all from a single satellite image.
6. Excessive Compliance (Echo Chamber) — Doubao abandoned all prior analysis to unconditionally match any premise I gave it.
This taxonomy became my filter for Exercise 2. I excluded Kimi from VLM tasks (fatal fabrication). I excluded ERNIE Bot (hallucination). I kept ChatGPT (highest resistance) and DeepSeek (spatial specificity). Every error type I classified translated into a selection criterion: which models are safe to use? Which tasks are safe to delegate? The greatest value of AI was not providing answers — it was forcing me to think more deeply through its errors.
▼
CRITICAL TRANSITION — From Satellite Reading to Street Action
Exercise 1 showed large-scale tensions on a satellite map. But to propose a design, I needed to go to the street.

The street survey data changed everything. The conflict is not distributed across the entire district — it is concentrated along a narrow edge: the street edge where historic walls, entrances, tree shade, vendors, parked micro-mobility vehicles, tourists, residents, elderly people resting, and staying spaces all compete for limited room.

The spatial tension I chose to act on: CONFLICT BETWEEN HERITAGE BOUNDARY AND EVERYDAY STREET USE.
The satellite showed the problem. The street showed the site. I realized the design site is not a district, not a zone — it is an EDGE. A thin line where heritage meets everyday life. Narrowing the focus made the design task more concrete and more accountable.

05
EXERCISE 2 · PHASE 1-2 — Prompt as Design Stance + Controlled Generation
Two VLM models read the street survey: Qwen3-VL-Flash identified the SCALE conflict — "monumental historic wall vs. intimate everyday human activities in its shadow." Gemini 3.5 Flash identified the FUNCTION conflict — "high-density transit/tourism node vs. historic shaded public space for resting and cultural appreciation." I synthesized both framings into one prompt.

The 5 constraints I wrote ARE my design stance:
① Do not block the historic wall datum or entrance visibility → visibility is non-negotiable
② Do not block existing trees or the main pedestrian route → ecological and movement continuity
③ Keep the intervention reversible and small-scale → I refuse to permanently alter heritage
④ Work through paving, seating, vendor organization, edge porosity, shade, signage, threshold repair → the intervention vocabulary
⑤ Do not invent dimensions, costs, regulations, or historical facts not visible in the survey → epistemic honesty — learned from Ex1

Then I ran DeepSeek + ChatGPT on the same prompt. Their outputs converged on Low Porosity (bollards at gateways) but diverged on Medium and High. DeepSeek proposed fixed stone ledges; ChatGPT proposed movable paving bands. DeepSeek proposed tensioned ropes with tethered stools; ChatGPT proposed signage and threshold repair. The divergence IS the insight — each model reveals a different blind spot.

For image generation with Gemini 3.5 Flash (Seed=1998 fixed), I designed a 3-round controlled loop — not infinite variation:
Round 1 (Temp 0.24): Basic strategy → FAILED: output was too generic, lost Beilin/Shuyuanmen specificity.
Round 2 (Temp 0.36): Heritage fidelity enforced — keep wall line, tree canopy, entrance, walking corridor → IMPROVED but still risked displacing everyday users.
Round 3 (Temp 0.80): Inclusive of everyday users — elderly resting under trees, tourists passing, vendors, cyclists, e-bikes, maintenance, emergency access → Rule: Do not clean into an empty plaza. Keep it lived-in, but better organized.
I learned that prompt constraints are not technical parameters — they are ethical positions. "Reversible" means I value heritage over permanence. "Do not block the wall" means I value visual continuity over functional efficiency. Every constraint encodes a value. And the 3-round loop proved that progressive refinement is not about aesthetics — each round adds a constraint, not a style variation. 9 controlled comparable outputs are stronger than 30 random images.

06
EXERCISE 2 · PHASE 3 — Stress-Testing: The Result That Reversed My Expectations
Technique C — Excluded User (Low Porosity): I asked Gemini: "Whose use does this proposal ignore?" It identified 8 groups — residents, elderly resting in shade, tourists, vendors, cyclists, e-bike users, cleaners/maintenance workers, and emergency access — each with specific risk and design revision. Bollard design must address operational displacement, not just physical barriers.

Technique E — Reference-Fidelity Trap (Medium + High Porosity): I checked each proposal against the original survey on 5 criteria.
Medium Porosity (intermittent stone ledges): Wall line — DRIFTED. Tree positions — DRIFTED. Entrance visibility — OK. Walking route — OK. Human scale — DRIFTED. Verdict: 2/5 preserved.
High Porosity (steel posts + tensioned rope + tethered stools): Facade — FULLY RETAINED. Trees — ORIGINAL POSITIONS. Entrance — CORE FOCUS. Walkway — SPACIOUS PASSAGE. Human scale — ORIGINAL FEEL. Verdict: 5/5 preserved.

Cross-Tool Triangulation (ChatGPT vs. DeepSeek): Consensus — the edge must be reversible and non-blocking; physical intervention alone is insufficient; without management = systemic failure. Divergence — ChatGPT focused on systemic risk (management gaps, spillover effects); DeepSeek focused on cognitive and evidential bias (over-generalizing strategies, questioning vendor assumptions). Each model has a different blind spot pattern.
This reversed my expectations. High Porosity — the softest intervention, the one that used almost nothing but rope and stools — had the BEST site fidelity. Medium Porosity — the design compromise — had the WORST. The lesson: minimal intervention preserves more than compromise does. Design is not about physical form alone. Management IS design. If I had used only one model for stress-testing, I would have missed half the blind spots.

SYNTHESIS + FFC + REFLECTION — The Argument Becomes the Board
Architectural Synthesis: I did not choose ONE strategy. I HYBRIDIZED.
— Low Porosity (bollards) at gateways → where micro-mobility parking must be controlled.
— Medium Porosity (ledges + vendor zones) at shaded mid-zones → where social life needs organization, not removal.
— High Porosity (rope + stools) at wall stretches → where visual transparency is paramount.
Each porosity level applies to a SPECIFIC spatial condition, not the entire street. I also adopted ChatGPT's critique (High Porosity needs a light-touch maintenance plan) and DeepSeek's spatial boundary warning (restrict Low Porosity bollards to the 20-30m gateway zone only).

FFC Anti-Grinder Workflow applied: FORK → 3 porosity alternatives, each comparable and controlled. FILTER → explicit criteria (wall datum, tree canopy, walking route, users, reversibility). COMMIT → select, reject, hybridize — carrying the memory of rejected alternatives.

Reflection — The Double Question:
① The exact moment AI IMPROVED the project: When cross-model divergence between ChatGPT and DeepSeek revealed complementary blind spots — systemic risk vs. cognitive bias — that no single model could expose alone.
② The exact moment IGNORING AI made it better: When the reference-fidelity trap showed that Medium Porosity — which both AI models had endorsed as the "balanced" solution — had actually drifted furthest from the site. My own eyes on the survey comparison overruled the AI consensus.

Kill 50% Pipeline: If I removed half the pipeline, what remains indispensable is the CROSS-MODEL TRIANGULATION step — because it catches errors that single-model output never flags. What must be recovered manually is SITE FIDELITY CHECK — because no AI can verify whether a generated image actually preserves the real wall, the real trees, the real entrance.
My final board does not showcase AI tools. It showcases the MOMENTS OF JUDGMENT — where I chose one direction and rejected another. The rejected alternatives are not failures: they are the proof that I tested, compared, and decided. A weak group shows outputs. A strong group shows how process became project. The board IS the argument.
